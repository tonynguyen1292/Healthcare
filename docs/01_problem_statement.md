# Problem Statement

## Background

Mental health conditions are a leading cause of disease burden in Australia, affecting around one in five people aged 16–85 years in any 12‑month period. [web:5] However, fewer than half of those with a 12‑month mental disorder access a health professional for their mental health, indicating a substantial treatment gap. [web:5][web:9]

People in rural and remote areas experience similar rates of mental illness to those in major cities but face more limited access to mental health services, leading to higher avoidable hospitalisations, higher costs over time, and poorer outcomes. [web:7] In Western Australia, regional and remote communities report high distress, low wellbeing, and ongoing unmet mental health needs, especially among young people. [web:8][web:10]

## Core problem

There is a significant gap between the mental health needs of people in Western Australia and the services they receive, with financial barriers, geographic isolation, and system-level constraints contributing to inequitable access and outcomes. [web:7][web:10] While national and modelled datasets provide high‑level estimates of mental health prevalence and service use, this information is not always presented in a way that clearly highlights where the treatment gap is most severe or which communities are most affected. [web:5][web:12]

For decision makers, this makes it harder to prioritise funding, workforce, and service models that target the communities with the greatest unmet need.

## Project purpose

This project seeks to quantify and visualise the mental health treatment gap in Western Australia, using national and modelled estimates by state and Primary Health Network (PHN) to identify patterns of unmet need across regions and demographic groups. [web:5][web:12] It will translate these analytical findings into practical insights and recommendations that can support more equitable planning and investment in mental health services.

## Objectives

1. Estimate mental health need and service utilisation for Western Australia, using nationally consistent indicators from the National Study of Mental Health and Wellbeing and related sources. [web:5][web:12]  
2. Quantify the treatment gap (need minus service use) for Western Australia overall and for key regions (e.g. PHNs, metropolitan vs rural and remote). [web:7][web:10]  
3. Highlight populations and locations where barriers such as distance, affordability, or service availability are likely to be most significant. [web:7][web:10]  
4. Provide evidence‑based recommendations to inform policy, commissioning, and service design decisions that seek to reduce the treatment gap and improve equity of access.

## Success criteria

The project will be considered successful if it delivers:

- Clear, interpretable metrics and visualisations that show mental health need, service use, and treatment gap for Western Australia, benchmarked against national or other state/territory figures. [web:5][web:12]  
- A concise recommendation report that identifies priority regions and groups, explains the drivers of the treatment gap, and suggests actionable options for decision makers. [web:7][web:10]  
- Documentation that demonstrates a structured hybrid BA approach, connecting stakeholder needs and business requirements to data, analysis, and recommendations.
