# Business Requirements

## 1. Purpose

This document outlines the high‑level business requirements for the Mental Health Treatment Gap in Western Australia project. It connects stakeholder needs and the core problem to the analytical work and outputs that this case study will deliver.

The requirements are framed as if the project were commissioned by Western Australian health system decision makers, including state policymakers, Primary Health Networks (PHNs), and community mental health organisations.

---

## 2. Alignment with Problem and Objectives

### 2.1 Core problem

There is a significant gap between the mental health needs of people in Western Australia and the services they receive, with financial, geographic, and system‑level barriers contributing to inequitable access and outcomes.

### 2.2 Project objectives

The project aims to:

1. Estimate mental health need and service utilisation for Western Australia using nationally consistent indicators.
2. Quantify the treatment gap (need minus service use) overall and by key regions.
3. Highlight populations and locations where access barriers are likely to be most significant.
4. Provide evidence‑based recommendations to inform policy, commissioning, and service design decisions.

The business requirements below support these objectives.

---

## 3. High‑Level Business Requirements

### BR‑01: WA treatment gap overview

**Description**  
The organisation needs a clear and interpretable overview of mental health need, service use, and treatment gap for Western Australia, benchmarked against national figures and other states/territories.

**Rationale**  
State health planners must understand where WA sits relative to the rest of Australia to assess whether current levels of need and unmet need justify changes in policy, funding, or workforce strategies.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Summary metrics for WA (need, service use, treatment gap) are presented using nationally consistent definitions.
- WA metrics are compared with national averages and at least one other state/territory.
- Key findings are summarised in plain language for non‑technical audiences.

---

### BR‑02: Regional treatment gap insights

**Description**  
Regional and PHN planners need localised information showing how mental health need, service use, and treatment gap vary across Primary Health Network regions and metropolitan vs rural/remote areas within WA.

**Rationale**  
PHNs and regional planners require evidence on where gaps are largest to target commissioned services, outreach, and community supports.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Treatment gap indicators are available by PHN and (where data allow) remoteness or metro/rural classifications within WA.
- Regions with relatively high need and low service use are clearly identified.
- Visualisations and tables allow planners to compare regions at a glance.

---

### BR‑03: Priority populations and barrier profiling

**Description**  
Decision makers need to understand which populations (e.g. age groups, sex, selected demographic categories) and locations face the greatest barriers related to distance, affordability, or service availability.

**Rationale**  
Targeted interventions require insight into who is most affected and why, rather than only high‑level averages.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Analysis highlights at least two priority population groups and/or regions with high unmet need.
- Evidence is provided on relevant barrier types (e.g. geographic isolation, workforce shortages, out‑of‑pocket costs) drawing on available indicators and contextual sources.
- Findings are summarised in a way that can be directly used in planning or advocacy.

---

### BR‑04: Decision‑ready recommendations

**Description**  
Senior decision makers need concise, evidence‑based recommendations that outline possible options for reducing the treatment gap and improving equity of access in Western Australia.

**Rationale**  
Analytical findings must be translated into actionable options, not just descriptive statistics.

**Acceptance criteria**

- A short recommendation report is produced that identifies priority regions and groups.
- Recommendations are linked to the evidence generated in the analysis (e.g. data tables, charts, contextual reports).
- Each recommendation includes a clear rationale and indicates which stakeholder groups would be responsible for implementation.

---

### BR‑05: Transparent, reusable analytical workflow

**Description**  
Analysts and students need documentation and artifacts that demonstrate how national and modelled datasets were used to derive treatment gap metrics for WA and its regions.
**Rationale**  
Transparent workflows support future reuse, extension to other states or topics, and learning for hybrid BA/data practitioners.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Data sources, transformations, and analytical steps are documented and reproducible.
- Notebooks and processed datasets are organised and described clearly in the repository.
- The workflow connects back to stakeholder needs and business requirements, not just technical steps.

---

## 4. Constraints and Dependencies

- This project relies on aggregated, modelled datasets (e.g. National Study of Mental Health and Wellbeing tables) which may limit granularity for some regions or groups.
- Time and scope constraints mean the project prioritises a clear end‑to‑end demonstration over exhaustive analysis.
- Qualitative and contextual evidence is used to support interpretation of quantitative findings where direct indicators of barriers are limited.
