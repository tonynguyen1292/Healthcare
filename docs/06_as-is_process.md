# AS-IS Process: Accessing Mental Health Care in Western Australia

## 1. Purpose

This document describes a simplified AS‑IS process for how an adult in Western Australia typically seeks and accesses mental health support within the current system.

It is based on high‑level patterns reported in national surveys and public evidence rather than detailed local process mapping, and is intended to support gap analysis and future TO‑BE process design for more equitable access.

---

## 2. Scope of the AS-IS Process

### 2.1 Included in scope

- Adults (16+) in Western Australia experiencing mental health difficulties.
- Initial help‑seeking journey, from recognising a problem through to receiving some form of professional support (if reached).
- Interactions with:
  - Primary care (e.g. GPs).
  - Public specialist mental health services where relevant.
  - Community and NGO mental health services (at a high level).

### 2.2 Out of scope

- Detailed clinical pathways or treatment protocols.
- Child and adolescent mental health pathways.
- Crisis and emergency pathways (e.g. ED presentations, acute inpatient).
- Detailed funding and commissioning processes.

This scope is aligned with the project focus on the treatment gap (need vs service use) rather than clinical outcomes.

---

## 3. High-Level AS-IS Process Narrative

### Step 1 – Experience of mental health symptoms

1. An individual in WA experiences symptoms of psychological distress or mental disorder (e.g. persistent low mood, anxiety, sleep problems).
2. Symptoms may be influenced by personal circumstances, social determinants, and local service availability, with particular challenges in rural and remote areas.

**Pain points / risks**

- Limited mental health literacy or stigma may prevent recognition of symptoms as requiring professional help.
- In regional and remote communities, isolation and lack of local services can exacerbate distress and delay help‑seeking.

---

### Step 2 – Problem recognition and help-seeking decision

1. The person (or their family/friends) recognises that support may be needed.
2. They consider whether to seek help and what type of help (self‑help, informal support, GP, helpline, online service, etc.).
3. Factors influencing the decision include:
   - Perceived severity of symptoms.
   - Awareness of available services.
   - Perceived costs and travel effort.
   - Attitudes and stigma.

**Pain points / risks**

- Some individuals decide not to seek professional help despite significant need, contributing to the treatment gap.
- Awareness of free or low‑cost services may be limited, especially outside metropolitan areas.

---

### Step 3 – First point of contact (often GP)

1. For many people, the GP is the first professional contact for mental health concerns.
2. The individual books an appointment (if they have a regular GP or local practice) and attends a consultation.
3. The GP assesses symptoms, may provide brief interventions, and can:
   - Develop a mental health treatment plan.
   - Refer to psychological or psychiatric services.
   - Suggest community or online supports.

**Pain points / risks**

- In some regional and remote areas, GP availability is limited, leading to long wait times or the need to travel substantial distances.
- Out‑of‑pocket costs (co‑payments) can be a barrier, particularly for people on low incomes, even if some services are subsidised.
- Short consultation times can constrain thorough assessment and follow‑up planning.

---

### Step 4 – Referral and access to specialist or community services

1. If appropriate, the GP or another provider refers the person to:
   - A psychologist or counsellor (public, NGO, or private provider).
   - Public specialist mental health services (e.g. community mental health teams).
   - Other community‑based services (e.g. peer support, NGO programs).
2. The person attempts to access the referred service by:
   - Contacting the service to book an appointment.
   - Joining a waitlist if service capacity is limited.
   - Travelling to the service location or using telehealth if available.

**Pain points / risks**

- Limited availability of psychologists and psychiatrists in many regional and remote areas, and long waitlists even in metropolitan areas.
- Travel distance and time to reach services in remote WA, particularly where telehealth is not consistently available or accessible.
- Out‑of‑pocket costs for private providers, even when Medicare or other subsidies apply, can deter or limit uptake of care.

---

### Step 5 – Engagement with services (if accessed)

1. The person attends initial appointments and begins treatment or support.
2. They may receive:
   - Evidence‑based psychological therapies.
   - Medication management.
   - Community and psychosocial supports.
3. Follow‑up and continuity of care depend on:
   - Service capacity and funding.
   - Workforce stability.
   - Coordination between services (e.g. GP, public mental health, NGOs).

**Pain points / risks**

- Inconsistent availability and continuity, particularly in rural and remote settings with workforce shortages.
- Services may be time‑limited, with limited scope for ongoing support after funded sessions are exhausted.
- Coordination between primary care, specialist, and community services can be fragmented.

---

### Step 6 – No service accessed (treatment gap outcome)

For a substantial proportion of people with a 12‑month mental disorder, no professional mental health service is accessed during the year.

Reasons may include:

- Not recognising need or not wanting help.
- Perceived or actual lack of service availability.
- Financial barriers.
- Geographic barriers and travel burden.
- Previous negative experiences with services.

This outcome is the focus of the quantified treatment gap analysis in this project.

---

## 4. AS-IS Process Overview (Textual Flow)

Below is a simplified textual representation of the AS‑IS process:

1. Experience symptoms →  
2. Decide whether to seek help →  
3. Contact first point of care (often GP) →  
4. Assessment and potential referral →  
5a. Access specialist/community services and receive care; or  
5b. Do not access services (treatment gap).

Key cross‑cutting factors that influence each step include:

- Awareness and attitudes (stigma, perceived need).
- Financial capacity and insurance/benefit coverage.
- Distance to services and transport options.
- Local service availability and workforce.
- Cultural safety and appropriateness of services for different groups.

---

## 5. Pain Points and Barriers Summary

The table below summarises key pain points at each stage of the AS‑IS process.

| Process step                     | Key pain points / barriers                                                                 |
|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Symptom experience               | Low mental health literacy; stigma; lack of local informal supports                       |
| Help‑seeking decision            | Uncertainty about where to go; stigma; belief that services are not available or affordable |
| First contact (GP or other)      | GP shortages in rural/remote areas; cost; limited appointment time                        |
| Referral to services             | Long waitlists; limited local options; travel distance; inconsistent telehealth access    |
| Engagement with services         | Workforce shortages; fragmented care; time‑limited programs                               |
| No service accessed (gap)        | Combination of awareness, financial, geographic, and system‑level barriers                |

These pain points motivate the analytical focus on geographic and financial barriers, and system limitations contributing to the treatment gap.

---

## 6. Use of the AS-IS Process in This Project

The AS‑IS process will be used to:

- Inform the **gap analysis** between current access patterns and a desired state where need and service use are more closely aligned.
- Guide **selection of indicators** (e.g. geographic and financial proxies) that correspond to observed pain points.
- Provide context for **interpretation of treatment gap metrics** and formulation of recommendations in later documents and reports.

A future TO‑BE process (not in scope for this initial project phase) could describe improved pathways for priority regions and populations based on the findings of the analysis.
