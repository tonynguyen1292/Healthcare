# Scope and Assumptions

## In scope

- Analysis of mental health need and service use using national and modelled estimates for:
  - Australia overall.
  - States and territories, with focus on Western Australia.
  - Primary Health Network (PHN) regions where data are available. [web:12]
- Derived measures of the mental health treatment gap (e.g. estimated number or proportion of people with a 12‑month mental disorder who did not consult a health professional). [web:5][web:9]
- Descriptive analysis by:
  - Geography (WA vs other states; PHN; metro vs rural/remote where data allow). [web:7][web:10]
  - Basic demographic groups (age, sex) where provided in the source tables. [web:5][web:12]
- High‑level exploration of financial, geographic, and system-related access barriers using available indicators and contextual evidence. [web:7][web:10][web:13]
- Development of summary outputs:
  - Tables and charts.
  - Short analytical commentary.
  - A recommendation report targeted at decision makers.

## Out of scope

- Clinical evaluation of specific treatments or service models.
- Individual-level or patient-level analysis (project is based on aggregated statistics, not unit records). [web:5][web:12]
- Detailed economic modelling (e.g. cost‑effectiveness of specific interventions).
- Implementation planning for specific programs or services.
- Detailed analysis of subpopulations where data are insufficient or too aggregated (e.g. small remote communities where privacy protections limit granularity).

## Key assumptions

- Modelled estimates from the National Study of Mental Health and Wellbeing and related sources are sufficiently reliable to compare mental health need and service use across states and PHNs. [web:12]
- The gap between estimated need and reported service use is a reasonable proxy for the mental health treatment gap, acknowledging that some people may choose not to seek care even when services are available. [web:5][web:9]
- Available geographic classifications (e.g. PHN regions, remoteness categories) adequately capture the main access differences between metropolitan and rural/remote parts of Western Australia. [web:7][web:10]
- The most recent data release (e.g. 2020–2022 surveys and corresponding modelled estimates) provides a suitable basis for current planning, despite lags in publication. [web:5][web:12]
- Additional qualitative evidence (e.g. reports on rural access barriers and community mental health supports) can be used to contextualise quantitative findings and strengthen recommendations, even where datasets do not capture all dimensions of access. [web:7][web:10][web:13]

## Constraints

- Data availability and granularity may limit the ability to analyse small areas or specific groups (e.g. certain remote communities or intersectional subpopulations). [web:5][web:12]
- Time and scope constraints mean the project will prioritise a clear, end‑to‑end demonstration of the hybrid BA approach rather than exhaustive analysis.
